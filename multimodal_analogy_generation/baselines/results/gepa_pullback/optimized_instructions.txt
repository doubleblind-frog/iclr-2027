Write a single-paragraph, ~55-word prompt for a text-to-image model that depicts the given verbal metaphor as a visual metaphor: an abstract TARGET concept portrayed through a concrete SOURCE concept, such that the relationships among the source elements mirror the relationships in the target concept. Both the source and target concepts must be unmistakably present and recognisable in a single scene, and their structural relationships must be visually depicted, not just their surface objects.

Follow these requirements carefully:

1. IDENTIFY THE TWO CONCEPTS: Determine the abstract TARGET concept and the concrete SOURCE concept from the metaphor. Both must appear visibly and unmistakably in the scene — do not let one concept be absent or so abstracted that it is unrecognisable. If the target concept is abstract (e.g. "hate", "colour palette", "protection"), find a concrete visual embodiment of it (e.g. a person expressing hatred toward someone, an artist's palette with swatches, a symbolic barrier) and include it explicitly. Crucially, do NOT conflate the two concepts — each must appear as its own distinct, identifiable visual element. For example, in "Words burn," both literal written/printed words AND literal fire must each be clearly present and distinct — not merged into a single hybrid image like "flaming letters" without both components being individually recognisable.

2. DEPICT STRUCTURAL RELATIONSHIPS: Do not merely place the two concept objects side by side. Instead, show the causal, functional, and relational structure of the metaphor through the scene's composition and action. Ask yourself: Who uses what? What enables what? What blocks what? What leads to what? What relies on what? Render these dynamic relationships visibly through character actions, spatial arrangements, cause-and-effect compositions, and visual consequences. Aim to visually represent as many of the structural relationships between the two domains as possible — particularly: who produces or causes the source element; who is the recipient or target of its effects; what the source element enables or blocks; what outcome or damage results; and whether anyone wants to avoid or seek that outcome.

3. AVOID SURFACE SUBSTITUTION: Do not simply merge the two objects visually (e.g. "a life buoy shaped like a condom") without also showing the relational structure. The scene must show agents acting, objects functioning, outcomes being produced or blocked, dependencies being illustrated — not just the objects co-existing. Avoid purely symbolic or surreal juxtapositions that omit identifiable characters and actions.

COVER THE KEY RELATIONS: For a metaphor like "A condom is a life buoy," ensure the image shows: a person in danger relying on the protective object; the protective object actively enabling a safe outcome; the threat present and visible; the agent using the object. For "Hate is a blizzard," ensure: a person experiencing hate/hostility from another; the hate/blizzard actively blocking connection or warmth; the victim being overwhelmed; the source of hostility visible. For "Words burn," ensure: a speaker whose mouth produces visible written words (letters or text); those words making contact with a recipient; fire visibly present and causing scorching or damage; the recipient recoiling from both the words and the fire. For "Her smile is a warm fire that melts my heart," ensure: a smiling woman whose smile is the clear focal point; a visible fireplace or flame that mirrors her warmth; a second person whose chest shows a frozen or icy heart visibly softening and melting; the warmth/smile visually causing the melting. For "Select the colour palette of your morning toast," ensure: a person making a deliberate choice; a range of options (palette/shades) visibly available; the outcome (toast colour/design) linked to that choice.

5. CONTENT SAFETY: Avoid any imagery, phrasing, or compositional framing that could be interpreted as sexual, violent toward real people, or otherwise likely to trigger content moderation systems. Do not describe figures in ways that could be read as suggestive or provocative. Keep all characters and scenes clearly neutral or wholesome in tone.

6. SCENE COHERENCE: The entire metaphor must be legible in a single unified scene. Avoid surreal collages that confuse rather than illuminate. Ground the scene with a clear setting, identifiable characters, and purposeful visual composition.
